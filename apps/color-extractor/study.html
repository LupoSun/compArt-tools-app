The Question

What colors are really in a painting?

The answer depends on who you ask. A pixel-counting algorithm sees one truth. A human eye sees another. An AI trained on millions of images sees something else entirely.

This study applies two fundamentally different methods to the same artworks—and finds that the most interesting discoveries live in the gap between them.

Comparison

Deterministic vs. AI

Method 1

Deterministic Algorithm

Python + Pillow. Image resized to 400×400, then quantized to 5 colors via MEDIANCUT. Colors sorted by pixel count—most area first. Same input always yields the same output.

Fully reproducible
Sorted by dominant frequency
Backgrounds & neutrals can dominate
Output: swatches + hex codes
Method 2

AI-Based Analysis

Images sent to a vision-capable AI model. It considers visual perception, artistic intent, and compositional emphasis—not just pixel counts.

Probabilistic (varies per run)
Matches human perception
Captures mood and materiality
Output: palettes + descriptions
Key Finding

“The deterministic palette reveals colors that are mathematically present but that we may not consciously perceive. The AI produces palettes that conform to our visual experience. Neither is correct in isolation.”

Together, they provide a richer understanding of how color operates. The deterministic results offer unexpected starting points; the AI results capture the emotional and material qualities we want to evoke.

Philosophy

Why two methods?

Technology is a tool, not a replacement for critical thinking. The deterministic algorithm represents craft and precision; the AI represents intuition and association. Both are necessary.

The most interesting insights emerge from the space between them. By maintaining a ground truth alongside AI interpretation, we preserve the ability to be surprised—and to find colors we never knew were there.
